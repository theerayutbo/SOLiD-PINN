## Analytical current distribution

Independent reference: Fuller & Harb, *Electrochemical Engineering* (Wiley, 2018), ISBN 9781119004257, Ch. 5 section 5.4, pp. 98–101, Eqs. (5.31)–(5.37). Ch. 7 section 7.3, pp. 156–158 discusses capacity and SOC conventions.

For constant equilibrium potential and small-signal Butler–Volmer kinetics, set g=j0 F/(RT), K=kappa/sigma, nu²=a*g*L²*(1/kappa+1/sigma). Our coordinate y starts at the separator; textbook z=1-y starts at the collector. For positive discharge J and insertion j, i_e/J=w(y), aLj/J=-dw/dy. The implementation evaluates the closed-form hyperbolic solution and its derivative; w(0)=1, w(1)=0 and integral of normalized reaction equals 1.

Run `python scripts/verify_analytical.py`. Four cases (corrected baseline plus three synthetic conductivity stress tests), 3 meshes (8/16/32), both current signs, 2 solvers = 48 rows. Nonlinear solvers are tested in the small-signal limit at mean j/j0=1e-3, not assumed identical to linear kinetics at arbitrary current. FEM compared at nodes, FVM to cell-average reference; errors are normalized by absolute mean reaction. The 0.2% fine-mesh target is a project gate, not a published standard. Test boundary conditions, integrated current, reflection symmetry, uniform limit, refinement and material conversions. This does not prove time-dependent diffusion or full model validity.

Mass-to-volume conversion uses w_i/rho_i normalized over constituents and multiplied by (1-void fraction). Binder must be included if present. Usable SoC mapping requires independently justified charged and discharged occupancy endpoints. Helpers are available but not silently applied to the solver.
